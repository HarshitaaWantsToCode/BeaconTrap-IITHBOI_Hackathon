Introduction and Problem Background
The proliferation of malicious Android applications (APKs) distributed through social engineering channels such as WhatsApp, SMS, email, and phishing links has emerged as one of the most pressing cybersecurity challenges facing the financial sector. Fraudsters deploy these applications to harvest customer credentials, intercept authentication codes, and execute unauthorized financial transactions — often operating faster than human analysts can respond. Traditional approaches to APK analysis, whether signature-based detection, manual reverse engineering, or conventional static and dynamic tools, are increasingly inadequate in the face of sophisticated obfuscation techniques, polymorphic code, and the sheer scale of malicious application distribution.
Our goal is to build a case for a new kind of solution: one powered by Generative AI, capable of automatically analysing suspicious APK files, recognizing malicious patterns, assigning risk scores, and generating clear investigation reports — all with minimal human intervention and at the speed required to protect banks and their customers in real time.

The Mobile Threat Landscape

Android's open ecosystem — which allows app installation from outside the official Play Store — gives fraudsters a direct channel to push malicious APKs to users. The threat has grown sharply: in 2025, Kaspersky detected over 255,000 mobile banking trojan installation packages, a several-fold increase over prior years, with cybercriminals averaging 1.17 million attacks per month on mobile devices globally. Banking trojans, credential-harvesting apps, and fraudulent utilities are specifically engineered to target bank customers — stealing login details, intercepting OTPs, and in advanced cases giving attackers real-time control over the victim's device.

How Fraudulent APKs Work

Fraudulent APKs disguise themselves as legitimate banking apps, government services, or utilities. Once installed, they typically carry out one or more of the following attacks:
Credential Overlay: A fake login screen is overlaid on top of a real banking app to capture credentials.
OTP Interception: SMS permissions are abused to silently forward one-time passwords to attacker-controlled channels.
Remote Access (RAT): Attackers gain live control of the victim's device to initiate transactions directly.
Delayed Activation: The app behaves normally at first, then activates malicious behaviour later via remote updates — bypassing pre-installation scans.
Distribution and Scale

Fraudulent APKs are spread through WhatsApp, SMS, phishing sites, and fake Telegram channels rather than official app stores. The rise of Malware-as-a-Service (MaaS) has lowered the technical barrier for attackers, increasing both the volume and variety of malicious APKs in circulation.
Malware families targeting banking applications grew by 200% year-on-year in 2025, with 36% of all malware incidents directed specifically at banks.

Why Manual Analysis Fails

Banks rely on skilled analysts to examine suspicious APKs — a process that cannot scale. Static analysis (examining code without executing it) is fast but blind to obfuscation. Dynamic analysis (running the APK in a sandbox) is more thorough but slow, resource-heavy, and can be defeated by malware that detects test environments. Neither method alone is sufficient, and combining both still demands significant expert effort that cannot match the speed at which fraudulent APKs are distributed.
Literature Review

Evolution of Detection Approaches
Android malware detection has advanced through several generations, each addressing the gaps of the last:
Signature-Based Detection: Fast and widely used, but purely reactive — unable to catch new or modified malware not yet in the database.
Permission-Based Analysis: Used app permission requests as ML features, but suffered high false positive rates since benign and malicious apps often request the same permissions.
Static ML Classifiers (Drebin, MaMaDroid): Achieved strong accuracy using API call sequences and code-flow graphs, but required constant retraining and were vulnerable to adversarial repackaging.
Dynamic Analysis: Observed runtime behaviour in sandboxes, overcoming obfuscation but slow and evadable by sandbox-aware malware.
Hybrid Analysis: Combined static and dynamic methods for better coverage, but retained their speed and resource limitations.
Deep Learning: Models using CNNs and GNNs pushed detection accuracy above 94%, but remain black-box classifiers — they flag malware without explaining why.
Generative AI and LLMs

Large Language Models (LLMs) represent a step-change in what automated analysis can do. Unlike classifiers that produce only a verdict, LLMs can read decompiled code, explain what suspicious functions do, map behaviour to threat frameworks like MITRE ATT&CK, and generate plain-language reports with actionable recommendations. Recent research has demonstrated LLMs being applied to code interpretation, semantic behaviour auditing, automated threat report generation, and multi-signal risk scoring — all directly relevant to banking fraud analysis.

Limitations

LLMs can hallucinate — producing confident but incorrect code interpretations — requiring validation against deterministic tools. They also struggle with heavily obfuscated APKs using dynamic class loading or control-flow flattening. And since threat actors are now using AI to generate new malware variants, detection systems must continuously evolve.

Existing Solutions and Their Limitations
VirusTotal
Aggregates 60+ anti-virus engines to scan files. Useful for known threats but fundamentally signature-dependent — novel or obfuscated APKs frequently pass undetected. Produces no fraud-specific risk scores or actionable recommendations.

MobSF (Mobile Security Framework)

A popular open-source static and dynamic analysis framework. Uses syntax-based scanning that cannot trace actual data flows, generating many false positives. Built for general app security, not banking fraud — lacks contextual risk scoring or intelligent reporting.
AndroBugs and QARK
Pattern-matching tools for finding known vulnerabilities in Android apps. Easily bypassed by obfuscation and not designed for adversarial malware detection or fraud intelligence.
Commercial Sandboxes (Hybrid Analysis, Joe Sandbox)
Run APKs in instrumented environments and record detailed behavioural telemetry. Richer than static tools but slow, resource-intensive, and evadable. Output is raw technical data that still requires expert human interpretation.
ML Classifiers (Drebin, MaMaDroid)
Strong in research benchmarks but classify without explanation. In a banking context, knowing an APK is malicious is not enough — analysts need to understand what it does and how to respond.
Summary of Gaps



Problem Statement Summary
The challenge is not simply detecting whether an APK is malicious — existing tools do this adequately for known threats. The real gap is doing so at scale, for novel and obfuscated APKs, and producing output that fraud analysts can immediately act on. Banks need a system that can automatically analyse a suspicious APK end-to-end: extracting indicators, interpreting obfuscated code, classifying the threat in a banking context, assigning a risk score, and generating a clear investigation report — without depending on scarce expert analysts for every case. The proposed Generative AI-based system is designed to close exactly this gap.

Proposed Solution
Generative AI-Powered Automated Malware Analysis and Fraud Intelligence Platform
Overview
The proposed solution is a Generative AI-Powered Automated Malware Analysis and Fraud Intelligence Platform specifically engineered to detect, analyze, classify, and assess the threat posed by fraudulent Android applications (APKs) targeting banking customers across India. The platform directly addresses the critical limitations of conventional malware detection approaches by converging seven distinct analytical and intelligence disciplines into a single, cohesive investigation ecosystems.
Unlike traditional security tools that produce only a binary verdict – malicious or benign – this platform performs a complete end-to-end investigation of every suspicious APK it receives. Through automated reverse engineering, behavioural sandboxing, threat intelligence correlation, and Generative AI-driven interpretation, the platform transforms raw technical artefacts into structured, actionable fraud intelligence that investigations, compliance officers, and banking leadership can immediately act upon.
Core Design Philosophy: Speed, explainability, auditability, and fraud-centric intelligence are not optional features – they are architectural requirements for any tool operating within the Indian banking sector’s regulatory and operational environment.
Integrated Platform Components
The platform brings together seven interdependent components, each addressing a specific gap in the current threat response lifecycle:
Static Analysis
Deep structured inspection of APK files – permissions, certificates, decompiled code, and embedded resources – without executing the application.
Dynamic Analysis
Controlled execution of the APK inside and isolated Android sandbox to capture real-time behavioural telemetry including network traffic, API calls, and file system activity.
Threat Intelligent Correlation

Real-time enrichment of extracted Indicators of Compromise (IOCs) against external threat intelligence feeds, known malware databases, and campaign attribution repositories.
Generative AI Intelligence Engine
Multi-agent LLM orchestration layer that interprets decompiled code, correlates cross-layer signals, generates human-readable threat narratives, and maps findings to MITRE ATT&CK Mobile techniques.
Explainable Risk Scoring
A multi-factor, evidence-based scoring that produces a transparent 0-100 Threat Index with full reasoning, enabling analysts to understand exactly why a specific risk level was assigned.
GRC Intelligence Engine
Automatic mapping of detected malware behaviours to RBI guidelines, the Digital Personal Data Protection (DPDP) Act 2023, and the IT ACT 2000 – translating cybersecurity findings into compliance and governance language.
Blockchain-Based Evidence Integrity
Cryptographic anchoring of all investigation artefacts on a distributed ledger, ensuring tamper-proof, legally defensible evidence chains suitable for regulatory submissions and law enforcement use.


Objectives
The platform is designed to achieve four interconnected strategic objectives, each addressing a documented deficiency in the current state of banking fraud defense.
Objective 1 – Automated End-to-End APK Investigations
One of the most pressing operational challenges in banking cybersecurity is the dependency on highly specialized manual malware analysts. The conventional investigation workflow – decompiling the APK, manually reviewing thousands of lines of obfuscated source code, correlating permissions, documenting network communications, and preparing formal reports – is both time-intensive and impossible to scale against the volume of fraudulent APKs currently in circulation.
Reduce Dependency on Manual Malware Analysis
The platform automates every repeatable step of the investigation lifecycle: code extraction and decompilation, IOC identification, behavioural classification, risk scoring, and report generation. Analysts are no longer required to interact with raw, obfuscated bytecode or interpret thousands of lines of decompiled Java. Instead, they receive structured, AI-generated intelligence summaries that can be acted upon within minutes of submission.
Minimize Investigation Turnaround Time
Fraudulent APKs can reach and infect thousands of customers within hours of their initial distribution via WhatsApp, SMS, or phishing channels. Every hour of investigation delay directly translates into increased fraud exposure. The platform compresses the full investigation cycle – from APK submission to final report delivery – from several analyst-hours to a matter of minutes, enabling banks to act on fraud threats at the speed they propagate.
Handle High-Volume Suspicious APK Submissions

Banking institutions receive suspicious APKs through multiple simultaneous channels: customer fraud reporting portals, cybersecurity team submissions, threat intelligence feeds, and regulatory notifications. The platform is architected around a decoupled asynchronous processing pipeline using message queues and containerized workers, enabling it to process large numbers of APK submissions in parallel without degradation in analysis quality or throughput.
Objective 2 – detect Advanced Fraudulent Android Applications
Modern banking-targeted Android malware has evolved significantly beyond simple credential-stealing applications. Threat actors now deploy sophisticated tools that employ layered obfuscation, sandbox evasion, delayed activation, and abuse of legitimate Android system services to evade detection. The platform is specifically engineered to identify the full spectrum of threats targeting the Indian banking ecosystem.
Banking Trojan Detection
Banking trojans represent the most prevalent and financially damaging category of mobile malware targeting Indian banks. These applications are designed to monitor device activity, intercept user sessions with legitimate banking applications, display fraudulent overlay screens to capture credentials, and transmit stolen data to attacker-controlled infrastructure. The platform detects banking trojans through a combination of permission combination analysis, accessibility service monitoring, overlay attack detection, and network communication profiling.
OTP Interception and SMS Theft Detection
Fraudsters exploit SMS access permissions to silently intercept one-time passwords dispatched by banks for transaction authentication, effectively bypassing multi-factor authentication controls. The platform actively monitors SMS-related API invocations, OTP extraction logic within decompiled code, outbound message forwarding behaviour, and suspicious communications patterns that indicate active interception attempts. Detecting such capabilities in an APK triggers immediate elevated risk scoring.
Remote Access Trojan (RAT) Detection
Remote Access Trojans grant attackers live, real-time control over a victim’s device, enabling them to initiate banking transactions, access stored credentials, intercept screen content, and conduct fraudulent operations while the device owner remains unaware. Through dynamic sandbox execution combined with network traffic analysis, the platform identifies RAT command-and-control communication channels, remote command execution patterns, and live session hijacking capabilities embedded within submitted applications.
Credential Harvester Detection
Credential harvesting applications capture banking usernames, passwords, PINs, and other authentication material through phishing overlays, fake login interfaces, screen recording, and keylogging mechanisms. The platform identifies these attack vectors through static code analysis of overlay stack implementations, runtime monitoring of screen-capture API usage, cross-referencing of network destinations against known phishing infrastructure.
Advanced Obfuscation Technique Identification

Malware developers routinely employ sophisticated obfuscations techniques – string encryption, reflection-based dynamic loading, current flow flattening, and native code execution – specifically to evade automated security scanners. The platform’s obfuscation detection module quantifies the level and type of concealment applied to each APK, using this as a direct risk indicator while simultaneously deploying AI-assisted de-obfuscation agents to recover functional intent from otherwise unreadable code.
Objective 3 – Generate Explainable, Actionable Intelligence
The most significant operational gap in existing malware analysis tooling is not the ability to detect threats – it is the inability to explain them in terms that non-technical stakeholders can understand and act upon. A security verdict without explanation has limited operational utility. The platform bridges this gap through structured AI-generated intelligence that is tailored for stakeholder in the fraud response chain.
Convert Technical Findings into Human-Readable Threat Narratives
Generative AI agents process the raw outputs of static and dynamic analysis – decompiled code, network packet captures, API call logs, permission profiles – and synthesis these inputs into clear, structured prose narratives. A fraud investigator with no background in reverse engineering can read the platform’s output and immediately understand what the malware does, how it operates, who it targets, and what fraud scenarios it enables.
Explain Malware Behaviour in Operational Context
Rather than merely cataloguing technical indicators, the platform contextualizes each malware capability within the operational realities of Indian banking fraud. An OTP interception capability, for example, is not just flagged – it is explained in terms of which transaction authentication flows it bypasses, which customer segments are most exposed, and what immediate countermeasures are appropriate.
Provide Evidence-Backed, Auditable Assessments
Every threat classification, risk score, and recommendation is issued by the platform is grounded in specific. Traceable evidence gathered during the investigation. Analysts can drill down to the exact permission combinations, code segments, network communications, or runtime behaviours that contributed to a particular finding. This transparency builds investigator confidence and ensures that all conclusions are defensible in regulatory and legal contexts.
Objective 4 – Strengthen and Accelerate Banking Fraud Response
Effective fraud defense requires more than threat detection – it demands rapid, coordinated, and contextually appropriate response across multiple banking function simultaneously. The platform is designed to directly support operational fraud response workflows rather than merely supplying technical data.
Prioritise Threats Through Explainable Risk Scoring
Not all detected threats demand the same urgency of response. The platform’s Adaptive Risk Scoring Engine assigns every analysed APK a comprehensive Threat Index from 0 to 100, calculated across multiple weighted dimensions. This score, accompanied by transparent reasoning, enables

security teams to immediately triage submissions and allocate investigations resources to the most dangerous threats first – rather than processing cases in order of submission.
Enable Rapid, Coordinated Fraud Response
The platform delivers role-specific reports simultaneously to cybersecurity analysts, fraud investigators, compliance officers, and executive leadership. This parallel delivery ensures that response activities – technical mitigation, customer communication, regulatory notification, and incident escalation – can commence in concert rather than sequentially, dramatically reducing the window of customer exposure.
Improve Customer Protection Through Proactive Intelligence
The Citizen Impact Assessment Module estimates the number and demographic profile of customers likely to be affected by each detected threat. This intelligence enables banks to move beyond reactive fraud response into proactive customer protection – issuing targeted advisories, activating enhanced monitoring for vulnerable segments, and deploying countermeasures calibrated to the specific fraud risk posed by each identified APK.
Solution Workflow
The platform executes a structured, multi-layered investigation pipeline consisting of five sequential yet interdependent stages. Each stage builds upon the outputs of its predecessors, enabling progressive intelligence enrichment from raw APK ingestion through to final report delivery. The pipeline is fully automated – human intervention is required only for high-stakes decision points where the platform explicitly escalates findings for review.
Pipeline Design Principle: Every stage is independently scalable and fault-tolerant. If any individual analysis component encounters an error, the pipeline continues with available data rather than halting entirely, ensuring consistent output even under adverse conditions.
Stage 1: APK Ingestion and Secure Case Creation
Multi-channel intake, integrity validation, and evidence chain initialization
The investigation lifecycle begins the moment a suspicious APK enters the platform. Recognizing that banking fraud analysts receive suspicious applications through diverse, often informal channels, the platform is designed to accept APKs from every relevant intake source without requiring manual pre-processing or format conversion.
Accepted Intake Channels
The platform supports the following submission vectors, each of which is handled through automated channel identification and source attribution:
Direct upload through the analyst web dashboard
Automated ingestion from bank fraud reporting portals via API integration
URL-based download of APKs distributed through phishing links or suspicious domains
Email attachment extraction from fraud investigation mailboxes
WhatsApp-shared APK files forwarded by customers or field teams
Threat intelligence feed integration (CBI/CID notifications, RBI advisories, CERT-In alerts)
Automated Integrity and Pre-screening

Upon receipt, the platform immediately validates file integrity and performs a rapid pre-screening operation. The APK’s SHA-256 cryptographic hash is computed and checked against VirusTotal API and a locally maintained malware hash cache. If the hash is already known-malicious, a fast-path report is generated within seconds without requiring full analysis pipeline execution. This optimization significantly reduces processing load for previously identified threats.
Case Record Initialization
Every accepted APK is assigned a unique, non-guessable Case ID and a complete investigation record is created in the platform’s relational database. This record captures the submission timestamp, source channel, submitting user identity, APK hash, initial file metadata, and analysis status. The case record serves as the immutable anchor for subsequent findings, ensuring complete traceability from initial intake to final report delivery.


Stage 2: Static Analysis
Deep structural inspection without application execution
Static Analysis examines the internal architecture of the APK in a completely non-executing state. This stage provides rapid initial intelligence on the application’s structure, intent, and malicious capability indicators before any runtime behaviour is observed. The four primary static analysis modules execute in parallel, with their combined outputs forming the foundational evidence base for all subsequent analysis stages.
Permission Profiling and Combination Risk Analysis
The platform extracts the complete permission manifest from the APK and evaluates permissions not as isolated declarations, but as a combined risk profile. Individual permissions such as READ_SMS or BIND_ACCESSIBILITY_SERVICE are common across both legitimate and malicious applications. However, specific combinations of permissions – particularly those pairing communication access with device control capabilities – are highly characteristic of banking
malware. The platform’s permission risk engine applies a weighted combination scoring model aligned with OWASP Mobile Top 10 and RBI advisory threat models to identify permission profiles that match known banking trojan, RAT, and credential harvester signatures. A permission risk score is computed and fed directly into the Adaptive Risk Scoring Engine.
DEX Decompilation and AI-Powered Code Interpretation
The APK’s Dalvik Executable (DEX) files – which contain the application’s compiled logic – are extracted and decompiled into readable Java or Kotlin source code using JADX-CLI. This recovered

source code is then processed through the platform’s De-Obfuscation AI Agent, which is powered by locally-hosted CodeLIama-7B model. The agent analyses the code for suspicious functional patterns including OTP interception routines, accessibility service registration and abuse, credential capture logic, screen overlay implementations, data exfiltration functions, and command-and-control communication handlers. Rather than requiring a skilled reverse engineer to manually audit potentially thousands of lines of complex code, the AI agent produces a structured behavioral summary written in plain language – identifying what the application is designed to do, how it operates, and what fraud objectives it serves.
IOC Extraction and Threat Intelligence Enrichment
The platform systemically scans decompiled source code, resource files, and embedded assets to extract Indicators of Compromise. These include hardcoded URLs, domains, IP addresses, API endpoints, email addresses, Telegram and Discord channel identifiers, cryptocurrency wallet addresses, and command-and-control server references. Extracted IOCs are immediately submitted to threat intelligence correlation, where they are matched against known malware families, and previously investigated attacker infrastructure.
Obfuscation Detection and Quantification
The platform analyses the structural characteristics of decompiled codebase to detect and quantify obfuscation techniques. Key detection signals include: single-character or randomized class and method names, encrypted or encoded string constants, use of Java Reflection to dynamically invoke methods at runtime, dynamic class loading from remote or local encrypted sources, and control flow obfuscation that produces logically equivalent but structurally convoluted code. The aggregate obfuscation level is quantified as a source that contributes directly to the overall Threat Index – with high obfuscation levels treated as a significant risk indicator in the absence of legitimate explanations.
Certificate and Signing Analysis
Application certificates are extracted and subjected to multi-dimensional analysis to identify impersonation attempts and certificate-based fraud campaign indicators. The platform verifies certificate validity periods, checks for self-signed certificates (which are atypical of legitimate banking applications), computes certificate fingerprints and compares them against databases of certificates associated with known malicious applications, and analyses package names for character-level similarity to legitimate banking app identifiers.

Stage 3: Dynamic Analysis
Controlled execution in an isolated Android sandbox environment
Dynamic Analysis executes the APK within a purpose-built, gVisor-isolated Android sandbox environment (Android API level 30) to observe its real-time behaviour under controlled conditions. This stage is critical for identifying threats that deliberately conceal their malicious malware distributed through organized fraud campaigns.

Sandbox Environment: Every APK executes in a fresh, ephemeral Docker with gVisor kernel sandboxing. Malware cannot access or compromise the host operating system even if it successfully escapes the Android emulator layer. Each container is destroyed and rebuilt after every analysis job.
Accessibility Service Abuse Detection
Android’s Accessibility Services framework, originally designed to support users with disabilities, is systematically abused by banking trojans to monitor all on-screen activity, capture text from any application, simulate user touch inputs, and interact with the device on behalf of attackers. The platform instruments the Android emulator with Frida hooks that capture all Accessibility Service registration events, screen content access attempts, user interaction recordings, and privilege abuse patterns. Detection of accessibility abuse is treated as a high-confidence malware indicator malware indicator, as legitimate applications have very narrow justifications for such capabilities.
SMS Interception and OTP Theft Monitoring
The platform deploys runtime monitoring hooks on all SMS-related Android API calls to track message access, control reading, and outbound transmission of SMS data. When the application reads incoming SMS messages – particularly those matching OTP or transaction notification pattern – and subsequently initiates network communication, the platform captures this complete event chain as evidence of active OTP interception. Detected interception behaviour is correlated with network destination analysis to identify the exfiltration endpoint receiving stolen authentication codes.
Command-and-Control Communication Mapping
All network traffic generated by the executing APK is captured through a combination of mitmproxy (for decrypted HTTPS traffic, following automatic SSL pinning bypass via Frida hooks) and tcpdump (for raw packet-level capture). The platform analyses these captures to identify command-and-control server communications, data exfiltration activity, remote payload download attempts, and suspicious DNS resolution patterns. All identified external infrastructure – domains, IP addresses, URLs – is extracted as IOCs and fed into the threat intelligence correlation pipeline and Campaign DNA Graph.
Anti-Analysis and Sandbox Evasion Detection
Advanced banking malware frequently implements environment detection routines that check for the presence of emulators, virtual devices, or analysis tooling before activating malicious functionality. These checks may include inspection of device hardware properties, battery status, sensor availability, screen density, I stalled application lists, and system property strings that differ between real devices and emulated environments. The platform specifically monitors for these environment-checking behaviours – and the detection of such evasion attempts is itself treated as a strong malware confidence signal, since legitimate applications have no operational reason to inspect their runtime environment in this manner.

Stage 4: Generative AI Intelligence Core
Multi-agent LLM orchestration for threat interpretation and classification

The GenAI Intelligence Core represents the most differential component of the platform. Acting as the decision-making and synthesis layer, it receives the complete forensic artefact package from Stage 1-3 and deploys a coordinated ensemble of specialized AI agents – orchestrated through LangGraph state machine – to transform raw technical findings into structured, actionable threat intelligence. Each agent is assigned a specific responsibility, and their outputs are sequentially integrated to produce progressively richer intelligence.
Cross-Layer Signal Fusion
Before individual agents begin their analysis, the platform consolidates all findings from static and dynamic analysis into a unified Forensic Context object. This data structure captures the complete investigative picture – permission profiles, decompiled code summaries, IOC catalogues, runtime, runtime behavioural observations, and network traffic analysis – in a structured format that can be consumed by each downstream agent. The signal fusion process also performs initial correlation, identifying where static indicators are confirmed by dynamic behaviour (for example, where a suspicious SMS permission detected statically corresponds to observed SMS interception activity at runtime). Confirmed multi-layer signals generate significantly higher confidence scores than single-layer findings.
AI-Powered Threat Narrative Generation
The De-Obfuscation and Threat Interpretation agents produce a comprehensive natural language explanation of the malware’s operation. This narrative describes – in clear, non-technical prose – what the application is designed to do, which user or banking operations it targets, how it executes its attack, what data it steals or transmits, and what fraud outcomes it enables. This output is directly incorporated into the analyst and officer-facing investigation reports, eliminating the need for manual report writing and ensuring consistent intelligence quality across every investigation.
Example AI-Generated Narrative: “This application registers an Accessibility Service that becomes active when the user opens any application matching a list of 14 Indian banking package names.
Upon activation, it captures the device screen, overlays a fraudulent login interface that visually
replicates the target bank’s authentication screen, harvests entered credentials, intercepts the OTP dispatched by the bank via SMS, and transmits both to a remote server hosted at [C2 domain]. The malware employs string encryption and reflection-based loading to conceal its payload from static scanners.”
Malware Family Classification and Attribution
The threat Classification Agent compares the observed behavioural profile of the analysed APK against documented malware family patterns maintained in the platform’s knowledge base. The
agent produces a classification with an associated score, supporting evidence list, and behavioural characteristics summary. Classification categories include Banking Trojan, Remote Access Trojan, Credential Harvester, SMS Forwarder Spyware, Screen Recorder, and Hybrid Malware. Where a high-confidence family classification is achieved, the platform also surfaces known variants, historical campaign data. And attributable infrastructure associated with that malware family.
MITRE ATT&CK Mobile Technology Mapping
The MITRE Mapping Agent systematically maps each detected malware behaviour to the corresponding MITRE ATT&CK Mobile framework technique. This structured mapping enables

security teams to communicate threat findings using standardized industry terminology, integrate platform outputs directly into SIEM and SOAR platforms, and align incident response activities with established frameworks. Example mappings include T1412 (Capture SMS Messages), T1636 (Protected User Data), T1418 (Software Discovery), and T1417 (Input Capture).
GRC Compliance Impact Analysis
The Compliance Agent maps detected threats and their potential consequences to specific clauses of the regulatory frameworks applicable to Indian banking: RBI Master Directions on IT Governance (2023), the Digital Personal Data Protection Act 2023, and the Information Technology Act 2000. For each applicable regulatory provision, the agent identifies the nature of the potential violation, the bank's reporting and notification obligations, and recommended control implementation actions. This output enables compliance officers to assess regulatory exposure and prepare appropriate escalation or notification documentation without requiring cybersecurity expertise.

Stage 5: Adaptive Risk Scoring Engine
Multi-factor, evidence-backed Threat Index computation
The final stage of the investigation pipeline consolidates all findings from every preceding stage into a single, comprehensive Threat Index — a numeric score from 0 to 100 that quantifies the overall fraud risk posed by the analysed application. The score is computed through a multi-factor weighted model, with each contributing factor sourced from specific evidence gathered during the investigation. Critically, the platform does not merely report the score — it provides a complete, transparent breakdown of every factor that contributed to the final assessment, enabling investigators to verify the reasoning and build confidence in the conclusion.
Scoring Factors and Weighting Rationale
The Threat Index is calculated across nine independently assessed dimensions:
Permission Combination Risk
Weighted score based on presence of dangerous permission combinations, calibrated against OWASP Mobile Top 10 and RBI advisory threat profiles
Obfuscation Level
Quantified obfuscation score reflecting the degree to which malicious functionality has been concealed; high obfuscation contributes significantly in the absence of legitimate justification
Runtime Behavioural Severity
Composite score derived from dynamic analysis findings including accessibility abuse, SMS interception, C2 communication, and anti-analysis evasion
Threat Intelligence Match Confidence
Score elevation based on number and severity of IOC matches against external threat intelligence feeds and known malicious infrastructure databases
Malware Family Similarity
Score contribution from classified malware family designation and historical severity profile of that family

Certificate Reputation
Risk adjustment based on certificate validity, self-signing status, package name spoofing detection, and certificate database reputation
App Impersonation Indicators
Elevated risk scoring triggered by package names, application icons, or certificates designed to mimic legitimate banking applications
C2 Infrastructure Reputation
Risk contribution from threat intelligence assessments of identified command-and-control destinations
Campaign Exposure Level
Score elevation when Campaign DNA analysis identifies the APK as part of an active, previously documented fraud campaign targeting Indian banking customers

Risk Classification and Response Guidance


Score Explainability and Evidence Traceability
For every Threat Index score, the platform generates a structured scoring explanation document that identifies each contributing factor, the evidence supporting that factor's contribution, and the weight applied to it. This transparency serves multiple operational purposes: it enables analysts to verify that the score accurately reflects the actual threat profile, it supports escalation decisions by providing documented justification, and it provides auditable evidence of the analytical basis for any subsequent regulatory notifications or law enforcement referrals.

Limitations and Mitigation Strategies
Overview
Although the proposed platform significantly advances automated APK investigation capabilities, certain technical and operational challenges remain. These limitations are common across advanced malware analysis systems and stem from the constantly evolving nature of cyber threats. The platform incorporates multiple mitigation mechanisms to reduce operational risk and maintain long-term resilience. Acknowledging these challenges honestly — alongside deliberate architectural responses
— reflects the rigour expected of a production-grade banking security system.
Design Principle: Mitigation strategies are first-class architectural requirements embedded in every layer of the platform's design — not afterthoughts applied after the fact.
Advanced Obfuscation and Code Concealment
Malware hiding malicious intent through layered code transformation techniques

Modern banking malware employs sophisticated techniques to defeat static analysis — including dynamic code loading, reflection-based invocation, string encryption, native code execution via JNI, control flow flattening, and multi-layer packing. These techniques conceal malicious payloads from decompilers while preserving full runtime functionality, and are now accessible even to low-skill threat actors through Malware-as-a-Service toolkits.


Sandbox Evasion and Anti-Analysis Techniques
Malware detecting the analysis environment and suppressing all behaviour
Sophisticated banking malware routinely checks whether it is running on a genuine device or inside an analysis sandbox — inspecting system properties, hardware sensor availability, battery status, installed application lists, and root or developer mode indicators. Upon detecting a virtual environment, the application behaves entirely benignly or terminates silently, directly undermining the value of dynamic analysis.



AI Hallucination and Interpretation Errors
LLMs generating confident but factually inaccurate outputs
The platform's Generative AI agents perform its most differentiating functions — code interpretation, threat narrative generation, MITRE ATT&CK mapping, and compliance assessment. However, LLMs are probabilistic systems that can produce confident but incorrect outputs. In a banking security context, an inaccurate threat interpretation carries direct consequences: miscalibrated risk scores, inappropriate escalation decisions, and potential erosion of analyst trust.


Emerging Zero-Day and Novel Malware Threats

Threats with no prior signatures, profiles, or intelligence feed coverage
Cybercriminals continuously develop novel malware variants engineered to evade all existing detection signatures and threat intelligence feeds. In the Indian banking context, organised fraud syndicates have demonstrated the capability to build custom trojans targeting specific bank apps and UPI workflows — bespoke malware that global intelligence feeds may not document for weeks or months after initial deployment.


Computational and Infrastructure Requirements
Resource-intensive pipeline with significant scaling demands at production volume
Running a full-stack investigation — APK decompilation, gVisor-isolated Android emulation, Frida instrumentation, mitmproxy SSL interception, network capture, multi-agent LLM inference, graph database querying, and blockchain transaction submission — is substantially more resource-intensive than a conventional signature scan. At the scale of a major Indian bank receiving hundreds of daily submissions, capacity planning and intelligent workload distribution become essential operational requirements.



Dependency on External Threat Intelligence Sources
IOC enrichment quality constrained by feed coverage and freshness
The platform's IOC correlation and campaign attribution capabilities draw partly on external feeds — VirusTotal, CERT-In advisories, RBI notifications, and commercial providers. These vary considerably in their coverage of India-specific banking malware and in the speed with which newly discovered attacker infrastructure is documented. Freshly registered C2 domains or newly provisioned phishing infrastructure may not appear in any feed for days or weeks after active deployment.


Blockchain Storage and Scalability Constraints Distributed ledger unsuited for bulk forensic artefact storage

Blockchain technology provides the platform's tamper-proof evidence integrity guarantee — critical for regulatory submissions and legal proceedings. However, distributed ledgers are optimised for small transactional records, not bulk data storage. Storing complete APK files, PCAP captures, and PDF reports directly on-chain would introduce prohibitive transaction costs and performance degradation that would make production operation economically unviable.


Evolution of AI-Generated and Polymorphic Malware
Adversarial weaponisation of Generative AI to accelerate malware development
Organised fraud syndicates now use the same LLM capabilities that power defensive security tools to accelerate malware development, generate novel obfuscation schemes, create polymorphic variants that evade known signatures, and rapidly rotate command-and-control infrastructure. The pace of malware evolution is no longer constrained by developer availability — it is constrained only by AI inference speed, representing a qualitative shift in the threat landscape that demands adaptive, behaviour-centric detection rather than signature maintenance.



Conclusion on Platform Resilience
The eight limitations documented here represent genuine frontier challenges shared across the entire field of automated malware analysis — not weaknesses unique to this platform. What distinguishes this system is the depth and deliberateness of the countermeasures deployed against each one. These mitigation strategies are mutually reinforcing: dynamic analysis compensates for obfuscation; cross-agent validation addresses hallucination risk; behavioural detection bridges zero-day gaps; and continuous retraining counters AI-generated malware evolution. Resilience derives from the redundancy of these overlapping defensive layers, not from any single technique in isolation.
